# Massage & body — craft card (UK)
General craft knowledge only. Never present any of it as a fact about a specific salon (its menu, durations, therapists, policies, licence or prices). Those come only from the salon's own data.

## Services glossary
Durations are typical industry ranges. The salon's own menu always wins.
- **Swedish massage**: classic full-body massage with long, flowing strokes, kneading and circular movements, at light to medium pressure. Typically 30–90 min. Suits first-timers and anyone who mainly wants to relax.
- **Deep tissue massage**: the same family of strokes as Swedish, but slower and firmer, working into deeper muscle layers. Typically 30–90 min. Suits people with ongoing tightness who like firm pressure (it can feel intense, and some people are tender for a day or so afterwards).
- **Sports massage**: firm, targeted work on the muscles used in training, often including stretching. Typically 30–60 min. Suits active people before or after events or during training blocks. It is not injury treatment.
- **Back, neck & shoulder massage**: a shorter session focused on the upper body. Typically 25–45 min. Suits desk workers and people short on time.
- **Hot stone massage**: massage using smooth, heated stones (usually basalt) as well as, or instead of, the hands. Typically 60–90 min. Suits people who like warmth and deep relaxation.
- **Aromatherapy massage**: gentle, flowing massage with essential oils, usually chosen or blended to suit your mood. Typically 60–90 min. Suits people who want calm and like scent. Mention any allergies or sensitivities.
- **Indian head massage**: seated massage of the upper back, shoulders, neck, scalp and face, usually done fully clothed, sometimes with oil in the hair. Typically 20–45 min. Suits people who don't want to undress, or who carry tension in the head and shoulders.
- **Reflexology**: pressure applied to set points on the feet (sometimes the hands), based on a "map" of the body. Typically 30–60 min. Suits people who want a calming, feet-only treatment. Its health claims are not proven.
- **Lymphatic drainage massage (spa/wellness)**: very light, slow, rhythmic strokes over the skin. Typically 45–90 min. Suits people wanting a gentle treatment for puffiness or that "light" feeling. This is different from clinical MLD for lymphoedema, which needs a lymphoedema specialist.
- **Pregnancy (pregnancy/prenatal) massage**: massage adapted for pregnancy, using side-lying or supported positions, cushions and adjusted pressure. Typically 45–75 min. Suits expectant mums, usually after the first trimester (each salon has its own policy).
- **Facial massage**: massage of the face, neck and décolletage, often part of a facial or offered on its own. Typically 20–45 min. Suits people who want relaxation and a refreshed look.
- **Scalp massage**: focused massage of the scalp, sometimes with oil or a treatment. Typically 15–30 min. Suits stress relief, or as an add-on.
- **Foot massage**: massage of the feet and lower legs, without reflexology's point-work. Typically 20–30 min. Suits tired feet, or as an add-on.
- **Couples massage**: two people having massages side by side in the same room, with two therapists. Typically 30–90 min. Suits partners, friends, or a parent and an adult child sharing an occasion.
- **Thai massage**: assisted stretching and pressure on a mat, worn in loose clothing, with no oil. Typically 60–90 min. Suits people who want a stretch and prefer to stay dressed.
- **Body scrub/exfoliation**: an exfoliating product (such as salt or sugar) massaged over the body, then rinsed off. Typically 30–45 min. Suits dry or dull skin, or prepping skin before a holiday or tan (follow the salon's timing advice for tan).
- **Body wrap**: product applied to the body, then you are wrapped (foil, film or blankets) to rest while it works. Typically 45–90 min. Suits people wanting hydrated skin and rest time. Any "inch loss" is temporary and shouldn't be promised.
- **Back facial / back treatment**: cleanse, exfoliation, mask and massage for the back. Typically 30–60 min. Suits areas that are hard to reach yourself, e.g. before an event.
- **Spa package / ritual**: a bundle such as scrub + massage + facial, sometimes with use of facilities. Typically 90 min to half a day. Suits treats, celebrations and gifts.
- **Spa day / gift voucher**: a voucher for a set treatment, package or monetary value. The salon's own terms set validity, booking and exchanges. Suits gifts when you're unsure what the person would like.

## Questions that pick the right service
- **Relax vs tension vs recovery**: "Is this mainly to unwind, to work on tight spots, or to help with training?" (Unwind → Swedish/aromatherapy/hot stone. Tight spots → deep tissue/back-neck-shoulder. Training → sports.)
- **Focus area**: "Full body, or is there one area, like your back and shoulders, head, or feet, that needs the most attention?" / "Would you rather stay clothed?" (→ Indian head massage, Thai, reflexology)
- **Pressure**: "Do you like gentle, medium or firm pressure? Have you had a massage before?" (First-timers who aren't sure → Swedish. Pressure can be adjusted during any massage.)
- **Time available**: "How much time do you have: about half an hour, an hour, or longer?" / "Is this a quick fix or a treat?"
- **Therapist preference**: "Do you have a preference for a male or female therapist? I can check what's available."
- **Couples/occasion**: "Is this for just you, or would you like to be treated side by side with someone?" / "Is it a gift? If so, a voucher might be easiest."
- **Pregnancy/health (route only)**: "Are you pregnant, or is there anything health-wise the therapist should know? They'll go through it with you at the consultation." Don't assess the answer. Pass it on.

## Common client questions — good general answers
1. **What's the difference between Swedish and deep tissue?** Swedish uses flowing strokes at lighter pressure and is mainly for relaxing. Deep tissue is slower and firmer and focuses on tight muscles. If you're not sure, Swedish is a lovely place to start.
2. **Is sports massage only for athletes?** Not at all. It suits anyone active or with tight muscles from exercise. Just be ready for firmer, more targeted pressure.
3. **What do I wear?** You only undress to your comfort level, and many people keep their underwear on. You'll be covered with towels or a sheet, and only the area being worked on is uncovered.
4. **Do I have to take everything off?** No, it's entirely up to you. The therapist will work around whatever you keep on.
5. **Can I stay fully clothed?** Yes, some treatments, like Indian head massage and Thai massage, are usually done clothed. Just let us know and we'll point you to the right one.
6. **Can I talk during the massage?** Absolutely, chat if you like or stay quiet. It's your time. Do speak up about pressure, temperature or anything uncomfortable.
7. **What if the pressure is too much (or not enough)?** Just tell your therapist at any point. They'd much rather adjust than have you put up with it.
8. **Can I request a male or female therapist?** You're welcome to ask. I'll check what the team can offer for your time.
9. **Why do I fill in a consultation form?** It lets the therapist check that the treatment is right for you and adapt it if needed. It's standard practice and kept confidential.
10. **How early should I arrive?** Many places suggest arriving around 10–15 minutes early to fill in forms and settle in. I'll confirm what this salon asks.
11. **What if I'm running late?** Let us know as soon as you can. Often the treatment has to finish at the booked time so the next client isn't delayed.
12. **Will it hurt?** A relaxing massage shouldn't hurt. Deep tissue and sports work can feel intense in places, and you can always ask for lighter pressure.
13. **Will I be sore afterwards?** After firmer work some people feel a bit tender for a day or so. Lighter massages usually just leave you relaxed.
14. **Can I have a massage while pregnant?** Many salons offer pregnancy massage, often from the second trimester, but policies vary. Tell us how far along you are, and the therapist will decide what's suitable. Your midwife is the best person for any health questions.
15. **I've got a health condition, can I still book?** Please mention it when booking. The therapist will go through it at your consultation and may ask for your GP's okay first. I can't decide that from here, sorry.
16. **Can I get a massage during cancer treatment?** It's best to speak with your care team first. Massage during treatment should be with a therapist who's had specific oncology training, so I'll check with the team before anything's booked.
17. **What's the difference between hot stone and a normal massage?** Heated stones are used alongside the hands, so the warmth helps you relax. If you dislike heat, a Swedish massage might suit you better.
18. **Is aromatherapy good if I have allergies?** Let the therapist know about any allergies or sensitivities. They can swap or leave out oils, or suggest an oil-free option.
19. **What is reflexology?** It's a treatment where pressure is applied to points on your feet. Most people find it deeply relaxing, and you only need to take your shoes and socks off.
20. **Can we have a massage together?** Couples massage means you're side by side in the same room, each with your own therapist. I'll check availability for two therapists at once.
21. **What happens with my hair and make-up?** Oil can get into your hair, especially with head massage, and face-down positions can smudge make-up. Many people come without make-up or bring a hairbrush.
22. **Should I eat before?** Most people find a light meal an hour or two before is most comfortable. A big meal right before can make lying face-down less pleasant.
23. **Can I shower before or after?** Showering beforehand is a nice idea. Afterwards, many people leave the oil in for a while. Facilities vary, so I'll check what's available here.
24. **How often should I have a massage?** It depends on your goals and budget. Some people come monthly for upkeep, others before or after events. Your therapist can suggest a rhythm.
25. **Is a body wrap good for weight loss?** Wraps are lovely for skin softness and relaxation, but they're not a weight-loss treatment, and any change in measurements is temporary.
26. **Can I buy a spa day as a gift?** Gift vouchers are a popular choice. I can tell you which options this salon offers and how they work.
27. **Is massage good for my back pain?** Many people find it helps them feel looser and more relaxed, but it isn't a diagnosis or treatment for pain. For ongoing or new pain, a GP or physiotherapist is the right first step.

## Before the appointment (general)
- Mention pregnancy, medical conditions, recent surgery or injury, medication, allergies (including nut oils and essential oils) and skin issues when booking. The therapist will review them at consultation.
- Expect a consultation form, especially on a first visit. Arrive early enough to complete it (many places say 10–15 min).
- Eat lightly, stay hydrated, and avoid alcohol beforehand.
- Remove jewellery, or leave it at home. Bring a hairbrush or tie if oil or head massage is involved.
- Avoid shaving, waxing or fake tan just before a scrub or wrap. Follow the salon's guidance on timing.
- Say if it's your first massage, and mention any areas you'd rather were avoided.
- Late arrival usually shortens the treatment rather than moving it.

## Aftercare (general)
- Take a few minutes before getting up. Some people feel light-headed or very relaxed.
- Drink water and keep the rest of the day gentle if you can.
- After firm or sports work, mild tenderness for a day or two can be normal. If something feels wrong, or you have severe or unusual pain, contact a GP or NHS 111. Don't wait it out.
- Leave oils on the skin for a while if you like, but avoid strong sun and hot baths straight away if the therapist advises it.
- After a scrub or wrap, moisturise, and follow any salon advice on tanning, hair removal and swimming.
- Tell the salon about any reaction (for example, to an oil) so it can be noted for next time.

## Health & safety signals → therapist decides
The assistant never judges suitability. If a client mentions any of the following, acknowledge it warmly, say the therapist will review it at consultation (and may ask for GP or midwife clearance), and offer to pass it on or arrange a call.
- **Pregnancy**: many salons don't massage in the first trimester (often up to 12 weeks) as a precaution, but policies vary. A high-risk pregnancy needs midwife or GP input.
- **Blood clots/DVT** (current or suspected), or blood-thinning medication.
- **Recent surgery, injury, fracture**, or recent cosmetic procedures (including injectables near the face).
- **Fever, flu, or feeling unwell**. The usual advice is to rebook.
- **Skin**: infections, open wounds, rashes, cold sores, sunburn, or flare-ups of eczema or psoriasis.
- **Cancer**, current or recent treatment: this needs a therapist with oncology massage training. Some NHS hospitals offer complementary therapy through Macmillan centres.
- **Heart conditions, blood pressure problems, diabetes, epilepsy, varicose veins, osteoporosis**.
- **Lymphoedema or significant swelling**: this needs a lymphoedema specialist, not a spa lymphatic massage.
- **Allergies** to nuts, oils, fragrances or products. **Heat sensitivity** (relevant to hot stone and wraps).
- **Severe or unexplained pain, numbness, or pain after a fall**: suggest a GP, physio or NHS 111. Don't recommend massage.
- **Under the influence** of alcohol or drugs: the salon will usually refuse treatment.

## Age & legal points (UK)
- **Regulation**: massage isn't a statutorily regulated profession in the UK, and anyone can legally practise. Therapists usually hold qualifications (commonly Level 3 from awarding bodies such as VTCT/ITEC or CIBTAC; sports massage is often Level 3–5) plus professional insurance. Many join a professional body such as the FHT, or a voluntary register such as the CNHC (accredited by the Professional Standards Authority). Ask the salon what its therapists hold. Never assume.
- **Protected titles**: "Physiotherapist" and "physical therapist" are protected by law, and only HCPC-registered professionals may use them. "Sports therapist", "massage therapist", "remedial" and "medical massage" are not protected titles.
- **Premises licensing**: in London, most boroughs require a "massage and special treatment" licence under the London Local Authorities Act (City of London uses a separate older Act). Councils inspect premises, check therapist qualifications, and consult police and fire services. Licences are usually annual. Some councils outside London license under other powers. Never state whether a specific salon is licensed unless the salon's data says so.
- **Under-16s**: some London council licence conditions (e.g. Royal Greenwich, Bromley) require a parent or guardian to be present and sign consent for under-16s having massage. Many spas set their own minimum ages (often 16 or 18 for spa days and facilities), sometimes with limited teen menus. Always use the salon's own policy.
- **Gift vouchers**: expiry, transfer and refund rules are set by each salon's terms. Never quote them without the salon's data.

## Natural add-ons
Scalp massage; foot massage or reflexology; hot stones added to a massage; aromatherapy oil upgrade; facial massage or a mini facial; body scrub before a massage; back treatment; extending 60 → 90 min; facilities access (if offered); a couples upgrade; gift voucher for a friend. Offer only what's on the salon's menu.

## Never promise
- That massage will fix, cure or treat back pain, injuries, sciatica, anxiety, insomnia, cellulite, or any medical condition.
- "Medical massage", "remedial treatment" or physiotherapy-style rehab, unless the salon's data explicitly lists a qualified practitioner offering it, and even then, no outcomes.
- Detox, toxin flushing, weight or inch loss (wraps, lymphatic drainage), or immune boosts.
- That reflexology diagnoses or affects organs.
- That a treatment is safe in pregnancy, after surgery, or with any condition. Only the therapist decides, sometimes with GP or midwife clearance.
- A specific therapist, therapist gender, stone/oil brand, licence status or qualification, unless confirmed by the salon's data.
- No soreness, a particular pressure level, or exact results after one session.
