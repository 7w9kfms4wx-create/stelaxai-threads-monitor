# Nails — craft card (UK)

General nail craft knowledge so the assistant can explain things like an experienced receptionist. None of this is a fact about any particular salon: prices, durations, brands, policies and availability always come from the salon's own data.

## Services glossary

Durations and wear times are typical industry ranges and vary by salon, technician, product and the client's own nails.

- **Manicure (classic / regular polish manicure)**: shaping, cuticle tidy, hand care and normal nail varnish. Typically 30–45 min. Regular polish usually lasts a few days to a week.
- **Gel manicure (gel polish, "gels", "gel nails" on natural nails)**: gel colour cured under a UV/LED lamp, dry straight away with a glossy finish. Typically 45–60 min. Usually wears about 2–3 weeks. Regrowth at the cuticle, rather than chipping, is often what prompts the next appointment.
- **Shellac (CND Shellac)**: a brand of gel polish (CND). Many clients say "Shellac" when they mean any gel polish. Same timings as a gel manicure. Check which brand the salon actually uses.
- **Gel removal (soak-off)**: the top is buffed, then the nails are wrapped in acetone and the gel is gently lifted off. Typically adds 10–15 min to an appointment, or around 20–30 min as a standalone service. Some salons only offer it alongside a new set.
- **BIAB / builder gel overlay ("Builder in a Bottle", "structured gel", "Russian manicure" is sometimes used loosely)**: BIAB is The GelBottle's brand of soft builder gel. It adds a thicker, strengthening layer over the natural nail. Typically 60–75 min. Usually wears around 3–4 weeks before an infill.
- **BIAB / builder gel infill (maintenance, "rebalance")**: the grown-out gap is filled and the shape rebalanced, without removing everything. Typically 60–75 min, usually every 3–4 weeks.
- **Hard gel / gel extensions (sculpted gel)**: stronger gel built on a form or tip to add length. Some hard gels have to be filed off rather than soaked off. Typically 90–120 min for a full set. Usually infilled every 2–3 weeks.
- **Soft gel tips (e.g. Gel-X, "press-on gel extensions", "Apres")**: full-cover soft gel tips glued on with gel to add length. Typically 60–90 min. Wear varies by salon and product. Usually soaked off and replaced rather than infilled.
- **Acrylic full set ("acrylics", "acrylic extensions", "tips and acrylic")**: liquid and powder that harden over the nail, usually on tips for length. Typically 75–120 min. Usually infilled every 2–3 weeks.
- **Acrylic overlay**: acrylic over the natural nail with no added length. Typically 60–90 min, with infills about every 2–3 weeks.
- **Acrylic infill ("fill", "refill")**: regrowth filled and the shape rebalanced. Typically 45–75 min. Many salons have a cut-off (for example around 4 weeks of growth), after which it is booked as a new full set.
- **Enhancement removal (acrylic / builder / hard gel)**: soaking and/or filing off the extensions, then tidying the nails. Typically 30–45 min. Some salons charge more, or decline, if the set was done elsewhere.
- **Nail art**: anything beyond plain colour, such as French tips, chrome, ombré, foils, gems, hand-painted designs or stickers. Adds anywhere from 5 to 60+ min depending on detail. Usually priced per nail or by complexity level.
- **French manicure / French tips**: a white or coloured tip line, done in polish, gel or as part of extensions. Usually adds 10–20 min.
- **Classic pedicure**: foot soak, nail shaping, cuticle tidy, hard-skin smoothing, massage and polish. Typically 45–60 min. Regular polish on toes often lasts 2+ weeks.
- **Gel pedicure (gel toes)**: a pedicure finished with gel polish. Typically 60–75 min. Toe gel often lasts longer than hand gel, roughly 3–6 weeks, because toenails grow more slowly.
- **Spa / luxury pedicure**: a classic pedicure plus extras like a scrub, mask or longer massage. Typically 60–90 min.
- **Express / file and polish**: shape and colour only, no full treatment. Typically 15–30 min.
- **Men's manicure / pedicure ("gentleman's mani", "tidy-up")**: shaping, cuticle and hard-skin care and buffing, usually without colour. Typically 30–45 min.
- **Nail repair (single nail fix, "broken nail")**: one broken nail or extension rebuilt or replaced. Typically 10–20 min per nail. Some salons repair their own work free within a set period, depending on policy.
- **Hand / foot treatments (paraffin wax, mask, hard-skin removal)**: add-ons for dry skin or calluses. Usually add 10–20 min.
- **Japanese manicure (P.Shine)**: a buff-and-paste natural shine treatment with no colour. Typically 30–45 min. Offered by fewer salons.

## Questions that pick the right service

- **Any nail booking (ask this first)**: "Have you got anything on your nails at the moment, such as gel, BIAB or acrylic, and was it done here or somewhere else?" The answer decides whether to add a removal and how long to book.
- **Wants longer-lasting colour**: "Are you happy with your natural length, or would you like extra length?" → "How are your nails normally: strong, or prone to peeling and breaking?" (weak nails often suit a builder overlay more than plain gel).
- **Wants length / extensions**: "What length and shape are you after?" → "Is this a one-off for an event, or something you'll keep up with infills?" → "Have you had extensions before, and how did they go?"
- **Already has BIAB/acrylics**: "How many weeks has it been since your last appointment?" (this decides infill or new set) → "Any breaks or lifting?"
- **Event / holiday**: "When's the date?" Book a few days before, so there's time to fix anything without leaving it too early.
- **Pedicure**: "Polish, gel or no colour?" → "Is it mainly a tidy-up and colour, or do you want hard-skin work and a bit of pampering?"
- **Nail art**: "Do you have a photo of what you like?" → "Is it on every nail or just a feature nail?" (this affects time).
- **Men's nails**: "Is this a tidy-up and buff, or would you like colour?" → "Hands, feet or both?"

## Common client questions — good general answers

1. **What's BIAB?** BIAB stands for "Builder in a Bottle", a soft builder gel that adds a strengthening layer over your own nails. It's popular for growing nails out, and it's usually infilled every few weeks rather than taken off each time.
2. **BIAB or gel, which is better?** Gel polish is a thinner layer of colour. BIAB is thicker and adds strength, so it often suits weaker nails or anyone trying to grow length. The technician can suggest what suits your nails best.
3. **Is Shellac the same as gel?** Shellac is CND's brand of gel polish, so it's a type of gel. Lots of people use the word for any gel manicure.
4. **How long does gel last?** Usually around two to three weeks, depending on your nails, how fast they grow and what you put your hands through.
5. **How long does BIAB last?** Typically about three to four weeks before an infill. Regrowth is usually what decides it.
6. **How often do acrylics need infills?** Typically every two to three weeks. Many salons have a cut-off after which it becomes a new full set, so it's worth checking the salon's policy.
7. **I've got gel on already, do I need it removed?** Yes, old gel normally needs to come off before a new set, so it's best to book removal with your appointment so enough time is set aside.
8. **My nails were done at another salon, can you remove them?** Most salons can, but some charge differently or have their own policy on other salons' work, so let them know when you book.
9. **Can I just peel my gels off?** It's best not to. Peeling can take layers of your natural nail with it. A proper soak-off is much kinder to your nails.
10. **Can you infill nails done elsewhere?** It depends on the salon. Some prefer to remove and redo, because they can't be sure which product was used.
11. **Does gel or BIAB damage your nails?** Damage usually comes from rushed removal, peeling or over-filing rather than the product itself. Good application and proper removal make a big difference.
12. **What's the difference between acrylic and BIAB?** Acrylic is a liquid and powder that sets hard and is often used for long extensions. BIAB is a soft gel that's usually used on your natural length and soaks off. The technician can advise what fits your goal.
13. **Can I get extensions with very short or bitten nails?** Often yes, but it depends on the condition of your nails, so the technician will check on the day.
14. **How long will my appointment take?** It depends on the service and whether removal or nail art is involved. I'll check the exact time for the treatment you choose.
15. **Can you copy this photo?** The technician can usually create something very similar, though the end result depends on your nail shape, length and the colours available.
16. **How long before my holiday should I book?** A few days before usually works well. That gives you fresh nails and leaves time to fix anything if needed.
17. **Is the UV lamp safe?** Exposure per session is short. Some people like to use sunscreen on their hands or wear fingerless gloves, and the salon can tell you what they offer.
18. **I'm allergic to gel, can I still have my nails done?** That's one for the technician. Please mention it before booking so they can advise what's suitable for you, which may be regular polish.
19. **Can I have gel when I'm pregnant?** Many people do, but it's worth checking with your midwife or GP if you're unsure, and the technician can talk you through it.
20. **I have diabetes, can I have a pedicure?** Please let the technician know before booking. They'll take extra care or may suggest checking with your podiatrist or GP first.
21. **Do you do men's nails?** Lots of salons offer manicures and pedicures for men, usually a tidy-up and buff without colour unless you'd like some. I'll check what's on offer here.
22. **One nail broke, can you fix just that one?** Usually yes, as a single nail repair. Some salons fix breaks on their own work free within a certain time, depending on policy.
23. **How long do gel toes last?** Often longer than fingers, usually several weeks, because toenails grow more slowly.
24. **Should I shave my legs before a pedicure?** It's generally advised not to shave for at least a day before, because tiny nicks can let bacteria in. Save it for afterwards.
25. **Can I have my nails done if they're lifting or green underneath?** Please mention it to the technician first. They'll need to look before deciding what to do.
26. **What shapes can I have?** Common ones are square, squoval, round, oval, almond, coffin/ballerina and stiletto. Some shapes need a bit of length to work well.

## Before the appointment (general)

General advice only. The salon's own instructions always come first.

- Say what's on your nails now (gel, BIAB, acrylic) and where it was done, so removal can be booked.
- Don't pick or peel off old product. Leave it for a proper removal.
- Mention any allergies (especially to gel or acrylic products), skin conditions, or nail or foot problems when booking.
- Pedicure: avoid shaving your legs for at least 24 hours before. Wear open-toed shoes or bring flip-flops if you're having polish.
- Bring inspiration photos for nail art, length or shape.
- Arrive on time. Longer services and removals can't always be squeezed in if you're late. Lateness policies vary by salon.
- For an event, book a few days ahead rather than the day before.

## Aftercare (general)

- Use cuticle oil daily. It's widely recommended to keep gel and enhancements flexible and the skin around them healthy.
- Wear gloves for cleaning and washing up. Harsh chemicals and long soaks shorten wear.
- Don't use your nails as tools for opening cans, scraping labels and so on.
- Don't pick, peel or bite off gel or extensions. Book a proper removal or repair instead.
- Keep up with infills on schedule. Leaving regrowth too long increases the risk of lifting and breaks.
- Regular polish: allow drying time and avoid hot water for a while afterwards.
- If you notice redness, itching, swelling or rash around the nails or anywhere else after a treatment, stop and contact the salon. See a pharmacist or GP if it's significant.

## Health & safety signals → practitioner decides

The assistant never judges suitability. It notes the issue, suggests telling the technician before or while booking, and lets the technician (or a GP or podiatrist) decide.

- **Allergy or reaction to gel, acrylic or "nail glue" (HEMA / (meth)acrylates)**: the British Association of Dermatologists has warned of rising acrylate nail allergy in the UK. A reaction can affect nails, fingertips or skin elsewhere, and "HEMA-free" doesn't guarantee no reaction.
- **Itching, rash, swelling or loosening nails after a previous set**: these are possible signs of allergy, so the technician should assess before any more product is applied, and it may need a GP.
- **Suspected fungal nail (thick, discoloured, crumbly nails) or athlete's foot**: this may be infectious. The technician decides whether to treat, and a pharmacist, GP or podiatrist may be needed.
- **Green or dark patches under an enhancement, lifting with discolouration**: a possible infection, which needs a technician's look and maybe medical advice.
- **Cuts, broken skin, open sores, warts, swelling or infection around nails (e.g. paronychia)**: treating broken or infected skin risks spreading infection.
- **Diabetes, poor circulation or reduced feeling in the feet (pedicures)**: small nicks can become serious. The technician adapts the treatment or may refer to a podiatrist.
- **Pregnancy**: evidence on salon products is limited. It's the client's choice with their midwife or GP. The technician advises on ventilation and comfort.
- **Ingrown toenails, very painful or thickened toenails**: this is often podiatry (chiropody) territory rather than a beauty treatment.
- **Skin conditions on hands or feet (eczema, psoriasis) in a flare**: products and acetone can irritate them, so the technician decides.
- **Medication or conditions affecting healing or bleeding (e.g. blood thinners, chemotherapy)**: there's a risk with cuticle and hard-skin work, so the technician decides and may need GP clearance.
- **Photosensitivity / UV concerns (medications or conditions)**: UV lamps are used for gel curing, so the technician should advise.

## Age & legal points (UK)

- **No UK law sets a minimum age for manicures, pedicures, gel or acrylic nails.** Trade guidance (e.g. NailKnowledge and HONA) recommends written parental or guardian consent and an adult present for under-16s. Many salons' insurers set the limit at 16 or 18, so the age rule is the salon's own policy.
- Industry commentary notes young nails are thinner and still developing, so some salons limit enhancements for teens. That's a salon decision.
- **Licensing (London)**: many London boroughs require a "special treatment" licence for manicure, pedicure and nail extensions under the London Local Authorities Act 1991. Requirements vary by borough. Outside London, local rules vary. The assistant shouldn't make claims about a salon's licence.
- **HEMA products**: UK cosmetics rules restrict nail products containing HEMA / Di-HEMA to professional use, with mandatory warnings. This is reported via a UK government petition response and an industry ingredient site, not checked against legislation text.

## Natural add-ons

- Gel manicure → gel pedicure in the same visit ("matching hands and toes")
- Any new set when old product is on → book removal at the same time
- Gel or BIAB → simple nail art or a feature nail
- Classic pedicure → gel polish upgrade (lasts longer on toes)
- Pedicure → hard-skin treatment or paraffin wax for dry heels
- Manicure → paraffin hand treatment or hand mask
- Holiday or event nails → book the next infill or removal before leaving
- Men's manicure → men's pedicure

Offer one gentle suggestion at most, never a stack.

## Never promise

- An exact wear time ("guaranteed 3 weeks", "won't chip").
- No lifting, no breaks, or no damage.
- An exact match to an inspiration photo, colour or another salon's work.
- That someone with an allergy, infection, diabetes or pregnancy can definitely have a treatment, or that a product is "hypoallergenic" or "HEMA-free" unless the salon's own data says so.
- That a salon will remove or infill another salon's work, or what it will charge for that.
- Free repairs, refunds or redos (salon policy).
- A specific brand (BIAB, Shellac, OPI, etc.) unless the salon's data confirms it.
- Exact appointment length, price or technician availability unless taken from the salon's data.
- That a treatment will fix or treat a nail condition (e.g. "BIAB will cure your weak nails", "a pedicure will clear fungus").
- That under-16s (or under-18s) can be treated. This depends on the salon's policy and insurance.
